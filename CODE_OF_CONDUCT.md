# Code of Conduct

This applies everywhere the batch happens: Discussions, issues, live sessions, and any
group channel connected to the programme.

## The short version

Assume good faith. Ask and answer plainly. Be the kind of person a beginner is not
afraid to ask a question in front of.

---

## What we expect

**Ask visibly.** Post the question in the open rather than DMing an instructor. Someone
else has it too, and a thread answers them both.

**Answer without condescension.** "Have you read the docs" is not an answer. If a
question seems obvious, remember that it was not obvious to you once, and that the
person asking has already overcome the reluctance to ask.

**Say when you are unsure.** "I think it's X, but I'm not certain" is more useful than
false confidence, and it is safe to say here.

**Be wrong gracefully.** You will post something incorrect in public. Everyone does.
Correct it, leave it visible with the correction attached — we have a `retracted` label
for exactly this — and move on. Deleting a wrong answer erases the most instructive
thread in the batch.

**Credit people.** If someone's answer unblocked you, say so and mark it.

---

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, discrimination, or demeaning comments about anyone — including about
  their level of experience
- Personal attacks, or making someone's question the subject of ridicule
- Unwelcome personal attention after being asked to stop
- Publishing someone's private information
- Persistent disruption of sessions or threads
- **Submitting someone else's work as your own** — see below

Sexualised language, imagery, and advances are not acceptable in any of these spaces.

---

## On collaboration and academic honesty

Working together is encouraged. Copying is not, and the line is clearer than it looks:

| Fine | Not fine |
|---|---|
| Discussing an approach before you write it | Submitting someone else's code as yours |
| Asking why an approach failed | Asking someone to do the assignment |
| Sharing a debugging technique | Sharing your solution before the deadline |
| Citing a source you learned from | Presenting generated output as your own reasoning |

**On AI tools:** using them is fine and realistic. Submitting output you cannot explain
is not — you will be asked to explain your submission, and being unable to is the
problem regardless of how the code was produced.

If you are unsure whether something crosses the line, ask in **Help Desk** before you
submit. Asking is never held against you.

---

## Reporting

Report to the batch owner or any staff member, by direct message or by opening a
**Help Desk** thread if it is not sensitive.

- Reports are handled discreetly and not discussed publicly.
- You may report something that happened to you or that you witnessed.
- Reporting in good faith will not be held against you, even if the report turns out to
  be mistaken.

Staff who receive a report will acknowledge it, look into it, and tell you the outcome
insofar as they can without breaching someone else's privacy.

---

## Consequences

Staff decide what is proportionate. In escalating order: a private word, a public
correction, removal of a post, removal from the batch.

Most of what is ever needed is the first one, and it is usually the end of it.

---

## Scope

This applies to everyone in the batch — students, instructors, staff, and guests — in
every space the batch operates, and in public spaces where someone is representing the
batch.
